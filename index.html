    How one round works from start to finish

    Each round follows the same pattern. A short betting window opens, players place stakes, and the plane takes off while the multiplier climbs step by step. At a random point decided before the round begins, the plane leaves the screen and the round ends. Anyone who cashes out before that moment keeps the stake multiplied by the current value; anyone who waits too long loses that stake.

    Because the crash point is set in advance, nothing you do during the round can change it. That is why aviator predictor claims, aviator hack pages, and similar shortcuts do not actually work. Every round is independent, so the only real control a player has is the exit timing.

    Controls that matter on the screen

    • Two bet panels. Many versions let you place two separate stakes in one round, each with its own cash-out path.
    • Auto cash-out. Set a multiplier in advance and the game exits the bet automatically when that target is reached.
    • Auto bet. Repeats the same stake across rounds, which is useful if you want a steady session pace.
    • Live bet board. Shows what other players are staking and when they exit, but it does not predict the next outcome.
    • Round history. Shows recent crash points so you can judge volatility, not future results.

    A first round in five simple steps

    1. Start with the demo. The aviator demo is the safest place to learn the pace before using real money.
    2. Set a small stake. Keep the amount low so one bad round does not affect your full balance.
    3. Choose an exit target. Pick a number such as 1.5× or 2× before the round begins, or use auto cash-out.
    4. Watch the climb. The multiplier rises quickly, and the risk of missing your exit increases with every second.
    5. Cash out on time. Stick to the plan and treat anything beyond your target as gone already.
